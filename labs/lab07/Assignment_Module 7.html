This is the capstone of Stage 1. You will refactor the physics you've written across six labs into a single, modular engine — the World / Body / Force / Integrator architecture from this week's lesson. This engine is what you carry into the browser in Block 3 and build your final project on.

💡 The big idea this week: Good architecture turns "write a new simulation" into "assemble existing parts." When adding a force or a body takes a few lines instead of a rewrite, you've built an engine, not a script.

Part A — Build the Core Engine (35 points)

GUIDED — Follow the Lesson
  1. Implement the four classes from the lesson: Body, at least two Force types (e.g., Gravity and one of Drag/Spring/NewtonGravity), at least one Integrator (EulerCromer), and World.
  2. Include a force accumulator pattern: the world clears each body's accumulated force/torque, every force adds to it, then the integrator consumes it.
  3. Add a data-recording mechanism so the world can store the history of body positions (and energy/momentum) for plotting.
  4. Write a class diagram (image, ASCII, or a Mermaid diagram in Markdown) showing the four components and their relationships. Include it in your notebook.
💡 Hint: keeping forces general

Give every force an apply(self, world) method. The world just loops for f in self.forces: f.apply(self). It never needs to know which force it's calling — that's polymorphism doing the work.

Part B — Validate Against Your Old Labs (35 points)

GUIDED → INDEPENDENT

Prove the engine is correct by reproducing at least three earlier labs using only engine calls (no hand-written loops).

  1. Projectile (Labs 1–2): Gravity only; confirm the trajectory and energy behavior match your original.
  2. Oscillator (Lab 3): add a Spring force; confirm the period and phase-space ellipse match.
  3. Choose a third: collisions (Lab 4), an orbit with a Verlet integrator and NewtonGravity (Lab 5), or rotation (Lab 6).
  4. For each, overlay the engine result on your original lab result (or its known answer) and show they agree. Include the relevant conservation-law check.
💡 This is regression testing. You're using labs you already trust as a test suite for your new engine. If the engine reproduces all three with correct conservation behavior, it's sound. Professional engine developers do exactly this.

Part C — An Original Multi-Body Scene (30 points)

INDEPENDENT
  1. Build a scene your old scripts couldn't easily do: e.g., several balls under gravity bouncing in a box with collisions, a small "solar system" of multiple orbiting bodies, or a chain of springs.
  2. Use at least two forces and multiple bodies interacting through your engine.
  3. Track the appropriate conservation laws and confirm they behave correctly (conserved when they should be, dissipating only when physically expected).
  4. Produce a clear visualization (trajectory plot, multi-panel figure, or an animation if you're ambitious) and a short written description of the scene and what it demonstrates.

⭐ Bonus Challenge (up to 10 points)

Choose one:

  • Add a Verlet integrator and show your engine swaps EulerCromer → Verlet with a one-line change, then demonstrate improved orbit stability on the same scene.
  • Add a broad-phase optimization (spatial grid) and benchmark it against all-pairs collision checking for 100+ bodies. Plot runtime vs. N for both.
  • Create an animation with matplotlib.animation of your Part C scene and save it as a GIF/MP4 committed to your repo.

🏁 Block 2 Checkpoint — Engine Demo (separate submission)

The Block 2 Checkpoint is a short, peer-reviewed mini-project built on your Lab 7 engine. It marks the end of Stage 1 (Python) before you port to JavaScript in Block 3.

Requirements

  • A multi-body scene (at least 4 interacting bodies) running entirely on your engine
  • At least one collision interaction and at least two distinct forces
  • A conservation-law validation panel (energy and/or momentum) demonstrating correctness
  • A short README (in checkpoints/) explaining your architecture and how to run it
  • A 2–3 minute screen recording or a multi-panel figure sequence walking through the demo

Peer Review

You'll review two classmates' engines using a provided rubric focused on: (1) architecture clarity, (2) correct conservation behavior, (3) extensibility (how easily could you add a new force?). Peer review is required and has a firm deadline.

Why this matters

Whatever engine you submit here is the foundation you port to the browser in Block 3 and extend into your final project. A clean, well-validated engine now saves you enormous effort later.
