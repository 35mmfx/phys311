Part B — The Validation Report (40points)

INDEPENDENT — Highest Value

This is the most heavily weighted part of the week. Submit it as project/VALIDATION.md and summarize it in your README.

B1 — Headless convergence study (15 pts)

  1. Write project/convergence.js — no canvas, no rendering. It builds your scene, steps a fixed physical duration at several timesteps, and prints an observable.
  2. Run at least four timesteps (e.g. 1/60, 1/120, 1/240, 1/480) to a fixed physical end time.
  3. Report a table: DT, observable, error vs. the finest run, and the error ratio between successive rows.
  4. State the implied order and whether it matches your integrator (~2 for Euler-Cromer and Verlet, ~1 for Euler). If it doesn't match, investigate — that's a bug, and finding it is worth more than the points.
🔬 This test is only possible because of the layering rule. If engine/ is clean of ctx and document, this script is twenty lines. If it isn't, you'll have to untangle the renderer first — which is precisely the Week 9 warning coming due. Either way, do the test; the difficulty of writing it is itself a finding worth reporting.

B2 — Conservation results (12 pts)

  1. Conservative mode, long run: report |ΔE/E₀| over a stated duration and step count, with your DT and integrator named.
  2. Momentum: report |Δp|/|p₀| for an isolated configuration. Should be at round-off level.
  3. Angular momentum if your system has a central force or free rotation.
  4. Integrator comparison: run the same scene with two integrators and report the drift difference. This is your Lab 5 showdown, on your own system.
  5. Stress case: find the configuration where conservation is worst (pileups, near-misses, high speed) and report that number too.

B3 — Analytic limit + dissipation ledger (8 pts)

  1. At least one analytic comparison. Reduce your system to a case with a formula and report measured vs. predicted with % error. Explain any residual physically.
  2. If your system is dissipative or driven, include the ledger: ΔE compared against accumulated −∫F·v dt (or Δp vs. ∫F dt for thrust). Agreement to a stated tolerance.
  3. If inelastic collisions are present: confirm momentum conserved to round-off and KE monotonically non-increasing across many randomized collisions.

B4 — Limitations (5 pts)

A short honest section: where the model breaks down, the valid DT range, the body count at which performance degrades, physics deliberately omitted, and any known bug you didn't fix. Bound each one with a number where you can.

💡 Full credit here does not require passing every test. It requires measuring every test and reporting it accurately. A project that documents 3% energy drift in pileups, explains the cause, and bounds when it occurs scores higher than one claiming perfection with no numbers behind it.
💡 Hint: what if a test fails?

Good — you found a bug with a week to spare, which is exactly what this exercise is for. Work the Week 12 decision tree. Most Week 13 failures fall into four buckets: (1) a conservative force with no potentialEnergy(), so PE is missing from E; (2) Verlet reusing the old acceleration for the second half-step, which shows up as first-order convergence; (3) collisions resolved more than once per step, which shows up as energy gain on contact; (4) something in the loop keyed to wall-clock time rather than DT, which shows up as no convergence at all. If you can't fix it, report it precisely in Limitations.

Part C — Documentation (20 points)

DOCUMENTATION + PEER

C1 — README for developers (10 pts)

Write project/README.md following the lesson skeleton: pitch + live link, what it does, the physics with equations, the validation table, limitations, architecture, how to run locally, credits and references (including any AI assistance per the course policy).

C2 — On-page explainer for visitors (10 pts)

On the page itself, beside or below the canvas:

  • One sentence on what this is
  • A labeled controls list (keyboard and pointer)
  • "What to notice" — the physics payoff, in one or two sentences
  • A link to your README and repo
⚠️ The fifteen-second test is graded. A reviewer who lands on your page must be able to tell what it is, what to do, and what to look for — without scrolling and without reading your README. If your beta reviewers report confusion, fix it before submission; that's what the beta is for.

Part D — Peer Review (Discussion forum)

Review two peers --  Due December 2

Your classmates posted their betas at the end of Week 12.  For the two assigned to you and answer all three questions for each:

  1. Fifteen-second test. Without reading their README: what did you think it was, and what did you try? Report honestly where you got stuck.
  2. Stress-test the physics. Push a parameter to an extreme, stack bodies, set drag to zero, raise DT. Report the worst energy drift or momentum error you could produce, and how.
  3. Check a validation claim. Pick one claim from their README and try to reproduce it. Did you get the same number?
💡 Reviews are graded. A review that finds a reproducible physics bug, or reports "I clicked around for a minute and never figured out the goal," is worth full marks. A review that praises the color scheme is not. Try to break their simulation, that is the kindest thing you can do for a classmate this week.

Respond to your own feedback

Add a short project/REVIEW-RESPONSE.md: what your two reviewers said, what you changed, and what you deliberately declined with a one-line reason.
