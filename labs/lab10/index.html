Physics 311 · Lunar Lander

A fixed-step physics challenge: steer, harvest sunlight, and land softly.
Lunar lander simulation. Use left and right arrows to rotate, and hold up to thrust. Land upright and gently on the green pad.

Focus the canvas for keyboard controls. Hold left or right arrow (or A and D) to rotate. Hold up arrow (or W) to thrust. Solar reflectors collect sunlight most efficiently when the lander points upward. Land on the green pad with low speed and nearly upright.

Mission

Use the steerable electric thruster to slow your descent and reach the green pad. Solar reflectors recharge the propulsion reserve when aimed toward the sunlight. Touch down upright with a vertical speed under 1.8 m/s and horizontal speed under 1.1 m/s.

Lunar gravity is 1.62 m/s². Vacuum has no usable parachute braking, so this version uses an honest retro-thrust brake instead.

Focus the canvas: ←/→ or A/D rotate · hold ↑ or W to thrust and brake.

Physics notes. The lander uses fixed-step translational dynamics, lunar gravity, and a steerable thrust force. The energy readout remains visible, but is marked open because solar charging and thrust exchange energy with the lander; it is not a closed-system conservation test. Momentum is not conserved during powered flight. Touchdown is evaluated before the terrain collision response. The legacy scenes and their original integrator/conservation demonstrations remain available under “Other scenes and simulation settings.”
