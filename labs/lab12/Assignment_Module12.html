Part A -- The MUST Tier, Running (50 points)

INDEPENDENT

Build exactly what your proposal listed as MUST. Nothing more.

Required regardless of project

  1. Your scene builds and runs from project/main.js, using your Lab 9–10 engine/ and render/ modules with the layering rule intact.
  2. Fixed timestep via the accumulator. Physics must be frame-rate independent — verify by throttling CPU in DevTools and confirming behavior is unchanged.
  3. Your "one new thing" is implemented, or your documented fallback is implemented in its place.
  4. At least one user interaction works (keyboard, pointer, or a control) with a keyboard-accessible equivalent.
  5. Reset rebuilds the scene from initial conditions and re-baselines E₀.
  6. Deployed to GitHub Pages and verified on the live URL — not just on Live Server.
  7. No console errors on load or during normal use.
🔬 Deploy on day one. Push a deploy before the simulation is finished. Case-sensitive filenames, missing .js extensions in imports, and absolute paths all work locally and fail on Pages, and you don't want to meet them on Friday night. Deploy early, then every push is continuously verified.
💡 Hint: my Pages site is blank but Live Server works

Work this list. (1) DevTools Console on the live site — read the error. (2) 404s in the Network tab mean a path problem: check capitalization exactly (Vec2.js ≠ vec2.js on GitHub's servers, even though Windows and macOS don't care). (3) All import paths must be relative (./engine/vec2.js), never absolute (/engine/vec2.js). (4) Hard-refresh with Shift+reload. Pages caches aggressively. (5) Confirm Settings → Pages points at the right branch and folder, and that your project is in the published path.

Part B — Instrumentation & First Validation (30 points)

INDEPENDENT

You can't polish physics you can't measure. Build the instruments this week, while the code is small.

B1 — Debug HUD (12 pts)

  1. A DEBUG mode toggled by the D key or ?debug=1.
  2. Displays at minimum: total energy and drift as % of E₀, total momentum magnitude, body count, contacts resolved this step, max speed, and measured FPS.
  3. Drift readout turns red past your own stated tolerance.

B2 — Conservative test mode (10 pts)

A switch (checkbox, key, or URL parameter) that zeroes every dissipative and driving term — drag, thrust, friction — and sets e = 1. This reduces your project to a conservative system that must satisfy Block 1–2 standards.

💡 This switch is the most useful thing you will build this week. Every time your project misbehaves for the rest of the semester, your first move is to flip it on. If the conservative case is clean, your bug is in the dissipative terms. If it isn't, your bug is in the core loop. That's a two-second bisection of your entire codebase.

B3 — Run your two proposal tests (8 pts)

Execute the two validation tests you promised in Week 11. Report the actual numbers in your progress log, pass or fail. A failing test reported honestly is worth full marks here; Week 13 is when you fix it.

Part C — Check-In, Beta Post & Progress Log (20 points)

DOCUMENTATION + PEER

C1 — Midweek check-in post (6 pts)

By midweek, post to Discussion: Midweek Check-In:

  1. Your live URL (even if barely working)
  2. Which MUST items are done, in progress, and not started
  3. Your honest answer to the Wednesday rule: on track, or cutting something? If cutting, say what.
  4. One thing you're stuck on, or "nothing yet"

Then respond to one peer, ideally someone who's stuck on something you've already solved.

⚠️ The check-in is graded on honesty, not progress. "Two of five MUST items done, cutting the terrain generator to a hard-coded ramp" earns full credit. "On track!" from someone whose repo has no commits earns none. This exists so I can intervene while there's still time, use it.

C2 — Post your beta (4 pts)

By the end of this week, post to Discussion: Beta Review:

  1. Your live URL and a one-line description of what the visitor is looking at
  2. One thing you specifically want feedback on
  3. One thing you want stress-tested, a parameter to push, a configuration to try and break

C3 — Progress log (10 pts)

Submit project/PROGRESS.md (also paste into Canvas) covering:

  • MUST tier status table — each item marked done / cut / deferred, with one line of explanation for anything not done
  • Validation results — the table from B3 with real numbers
  • DECISIONS.md entries — every scope change made this week, in the three-line format from the lesson
  • Two bugs you fixed — symptom, cause, fix. One should be a physics bug, not a syntax bug
  • Next week's plan — the three SHOULD items you'll tackle first
🔬 The bug write-ups are real physics work. "Bodies gained energy on every bounce; cause was resolving the collision on consecutive frames because I never checked whether they were separating; fix was to skip resolution when the relative normal velocity is positive", that's a diagnosis, and it's exactly the kind of reasoning the final write-up is graded on. Start building the habit now.
